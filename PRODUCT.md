# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Anyone running a poker game, of any size: a kitchen table with a chip case and a TV, a weekly league at a bar, a charity tournament, a club night or a card room running several tables. The person running it might be a friend, a tournament director or a floor manager. Most show up cold, with no account and no tutorial, and need a game running before the first hand. PitMaster never assumes a home game or a casino: the same tool fits both, and the copy is written so either feels it was made for them.

Three moments carry equal weight:

- **Setup, before people arrive.** Picking the chip set, blinds, buy-ins and length, usually at a desk.
- **Dealing, mid-game.** The host, often one-handed on a laptop or phone between hands, busts players, logs rebuys, pauses the clock or cashes someone out. It has to be fast and hard to mess up.
- **The TV, at the table.** Everyone glances at the big screen from 10+ feet away, often in dim room light, to see blinds, time left, who's out and what's next.

## Product Purpose

PitMaster runs a poker game end to end, whatever its size: cash games and tournaments, a blind structure that fits the time you have, chip breakdowns from the real chips you play with, and a live TV view the whole room can read. It keeps the books too: who bought in, who won, who owes who, and, over time, a history of winners and results. It's a free side project with no accounts, and everything it keeps is encrypted on the host's device.

Success is a host who never opens a spreadsheet or calculator, a table that never asks "what are the blinds?", and a game that feels a little more like an event.

## Brand Personality

Raw, crafted, clutch.

- **Raw:** an "ugly website that looks good" in the spirit of are.na, tinybones.pages.dev and craigslist. Default-looking controls, dense info, hyperlinks that look like hyperlinks, no decoration for its own sake.
- **Crafted:** underneath the plain surface, the details are lovingly made. Chips are drawn to match the real set, the TV clock is tuned for a room, and small interactions feel considered.
- **Clutch:** casino drama is saved for the moments that earn it: a level going up, a bust, a color-up, the winner screen. The rest of the time it stays out of the way.

Voice: plain and short, like an experienced dealer: warm, never cute, never corporate. A little personality in empty states and banners, none in the middle of a task. Nothing assumes the size of the game, the currency or the time of day. Headings, labels and buttons are Title Case; sentences are sentence case. No em dashes.

## Anti-references

- **SaaS dashboard.** Rounded cards on cards, gradients, hero-metric stat tiles, Inter plus a purple primary button, "Welcome back!" chrome.
- **Online casino.** Neon, gold glitter, slot-machine flashing, jackpot fonts, anything that reads as a gambling app. Drama here is a moment, never a wallpaper.

## Design Principles

1. **Glanceable from the couch.** Anything that matters during play reads in under a second from across the room. Size, contrast and position beat decoration.
2. **Plain surface, obsessive details.** The shell stays raw; the craft goes into the objects that are real at the table: the chips, the clock, the blinds, the payouts.
3. **Earn the drama.** Motion, sound and color spikes are reserved for real game events. If everything flashes, nothing does.
4. **Never lose the money.** Buy-ins, rebuys, cash-outs and payouts are always visible, always undoable and always add up. Trust beats flair.
5. **Cold start, no manual.** A first-time host can run a full game without reading anything. Sensible defaults come first; power comes second.
6. **Any size, any room.** Every feature is its own switch, so a kitchen table and a ten-table room each take exactly the parts they need.
7. **Private by default.** Everything is encrypted on the device, nothing leaves it unless the host shares a TV code, and even then it's encrypted first. The legal pages say exactly what happens, and they change whenever data handling does.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Keyboard first-class:** every page is fully usable with Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, Space and arrow keys, with visible focus states and a logical tab order. The dealer's clock shortcuts must never trap focus or fire while typing in a field.
- **Readable across the room:** TV text is sized for 10+ feet in dim light, with high contrast and no thin weights for critical numbers.
- **Color-blind safe:** chip denominations, game states and warnings never rely on color alone. Always pair color with a label, value, icon or shape.
- Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast and interaction on the host-facing pages.
